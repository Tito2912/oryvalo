## Introduction

You don’t need a fancy website to get your first clients.

You need proof you can deliver.

That proof is a portfolio — but for beginners, the best portfolio is:
- **small**
- **specific**
- **easy to view**

This guide shows how to build an AI video portfolio in a weekend.

---

## What a portfolio needs to do

Your portfolio has one job:

**Make a business owner think: “This looks like something I could post.”**

It does not need:
- 20 videos
- perfect editing
- awards

It needs:
- a clear niche
- 3 examples that match what you sell
- a simple way to view them

---

## Pick one niche first

Portfolios fail when they are generic.

Pick one niche for 30 days:
- restaurants
- real estate
- gyms / trainers
- dentists / clinics
- local services

One niche makes everything easier:
- your formats repeat
- your scripts get faster
- your outreach feels relevant

---

## The 3 sample videos to create

Create 3 short videos (20–45 seconds).

These three cover most small-business needs.

### 1) Promo video (offer)

Goal: drive action.

Examples:
- limited-time discount
- new menu item
- free consultation

### 2) Social proof (trust)

Goal: reduce hesitation.

Examples:
- “5-star review” format
- before/after claims (careful with medical/finance)
- testimonial-style captions

### 3) Educational tip (authority)

Goal: attract attention without selling.

Examples:
- “3 mistakes people make when…”
- “1 quick tip to…”
- “what to expect when…”

If you sell a weekly package, these formats cover the month.

---

## Where to host your portfolio

Start simple.

Good beginner options:

### A single folder (recommended)

Create one folder named:

`AI Video Samples — {{Niche}}`

Inside:
- 3 video files
- 1 short PDF or doc called “What I deliver weekly”

### A private YouTube playlist (unlisted)

Works well if you want easy playback on mobile.

### A simple portfolio page

Build later once you have 3–5 clients.

---

## How to send it without looking spammy

Don’t DM strangers with links.

That triggers spam filters and looks desperate.

Instead:

1) Send a short outreach message  
2) Ask permission (“Want me to send examples?”)  
3) Then send the link  

This increases reply rate and makes you look professional.
